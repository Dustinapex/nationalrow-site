The Bell County East–Big Hill 765 kV Line Has Not Been Routed Yet.
Temple to Eldorado, Across 14 Central and West Texas Counties. Your Land May Still Be On It.

What the Bell County East–Big Hill study area crosses, county by county

Running west from Temple toward Big Hill in Schleicher County, ordered by how many of the 199 filed corridor links touch each county.

Bell County · Belton

The line starts here at Bell County East Switch, about five and a half miles southeast of Temple, and Bell carries 65 of the 199 filed links — a third of the whole corridor set. Blackland Prairie row-crop ground, irrigated bottoms, and a fast-thickening ring of acreage tracts around Temple, Belton and Salado. On land priced for its development horizon, damages to the remainder is usually a bigger number than the strip.

Williamson County · Georgetown

The most expensive dirt on the entire Texas 765 kV program. Georgetown, Round Rock, Hutto and Taylor are absorbing subdivision growth outward, and a 200-foot corridor across a tract with a platting future does not just take acres — it takes the part of the value that comes from what the land was going to become. Highest and best use is the argument here, and an agricultural comparable is not the right measure.

San Saba County · San Saba

42 filed links, second only to Bell. Pecan bottoms along the San Saba and Colorado rivers, hunting leases, and river-frontage tracts. Pecan orchards are a permanent planting: a corridor through one destroys decades of establishment, and the replacement cost of mature trees is a cost-to-cure item that a per-acre offer ignores entirely.

Burnet County · Burnet

33 links. Highland Lakes country — Lake Buchanan, granite hills, and recreational and second-home tracts that sell on view and privacy. When a transmission corridor crosses the ridgeline a buyer was paying for, the loss shows up in the remainder, not in the easement acres.

Menard County · Menard

28 links across Edwards Plateau ranch country on the Llano and San Saba drainages. Hunting lease income here is documentable and material, and a corridor through a pasture reduces the lease value of the pasture, not merely the strip.

McCulloch County · Brady

21 links. Brady sits near the geographic centre of Texas, and the corridors converge here. Mixed ranching, hunting and dryland farming, with wind and solar interest that competes with transmission for the same open, close-to-line ground.

Mason County · Mason

Granite and live oak ranch country with strong recreational demand and a growing wine and orchard economy. Small acreage and permanent plantings are the exposure: on a 40-acre tract a 200-foot corridor is a far larger proportion of the property than on a section of rangeland.

Schleicher County · Eldorado

The line ends here at Big Hill Substation, the same point where the Big Hill–Sand Lake project, Docket 59182, begins. A Schleicher County landowner can be inside both study areas at once, on two separate dockets, with two separate sets of land agents. Check the docket number on anything you receive.

Concho County · Paint Rock

Five filed links across farm and ranch ground on the Concho River. Small county, thin corridor coverage, but a tract inside one of those five links is exposed exactly like any other.

Lampasas County · Lampasas

Four links clipping the county. Hill Country ranchettes and grazing land within commuting reach of Killeen and Austin, which is why the acreage market here is stronger than the grass would suggest.

Tom Green County · San Angelo

One filed link. Tom Green also sits in the Big Hill–Sand Lake study area to the west, so a letter here needs its docket number read carefully before anyone assumes which project it belongs to.

Coryell County · Gatesville

Llano County · Llano

Also named with no filed link reaching it. Llano is surrounded by counties that are on the corridor set, so it is worth watching the docket rather than assuming it is settled.

Milam County · Cameron

Named in the case style, no filed link. The nearest corridors run through Bell and Williamson to the west and south.

Why the Bell County East–Big Hill line is being built

Electric load in the Permian Basin has grown faster than the transmission built to serve it. Oilfield operations that once ran on diesel and field gas now run on grid power, and large new loads — processing, compression, water handling, and increasingly data centers — have queued up in a part of Texas whose transmission backbone was designed for a much smaller demand.

The Legislature directed ERCOT to plan for that growth, and ERCOT’s Permian Basin reliability planning concluded that the region needed a step change rather than more of the same. The Public Utility Commission of Texas then decided the backbone would be built at 765 kilovolts — the first lines at that voltage anywhere in Texas. Bell County East–Big Hill is one segment of that backbone.

The practical consequence for a landowner is that this is not a project that gets cancelled. The need has been decided at the state level; what remains open in Docket 59475 is which of 122 filed corridors the Commission picks, and after that, what each affected owner is paid.

765 kV also means bigger hardware than most Texas landowners have dealt with. Oncor builds these on self-supporting steel lattice towers inside a 200-foot permanent easement — roughly five times the height of an ordinary distribution pole, on a strip about two-thirds the width of a football field is long.

A route being “preferred” in the application is not the route that gets built. On the neighbouring Longshore–Drill Hole project, the approved route was 17 of 119 filed links. Landowner participation in the routing record is a large part of why filed preferences move — which is exactly why the window before the final order matters.

What happens next, in order

Docket 59475 is the largest of the Texas 765 kV cases and it is close to a decision. Here is where it stands and what follows.

  • Done — 2025 into 2026

    Study area, public meetings and route alternatives

    Oncor and LCRA TSC mapped a study area across 14 counties, held public meetings and developed 122 alternative routes from 199 corridor links. Landowner comments filed at this stage genuinely moved routes.

  • Done — March 26, 2026

    Application filed at the PUCT

    Docket 59475 opened. The applicants asked for a certificate of convenience and necessity for a 765 kV line from Bell County East Switch near Temple to Big Hill Substation near Eldorado.

  • Done — April 27, 2026

    Intervention deadline

    The date by which a landowner had to formally intervene to become a party. That date has passed, which affects your standing in the routing case — it does not affect your right to be paid just compensation for a taking.

  • Through 2026

    Contested-case hearing

    More than 6,500 filings have been made in this docket, the great majority of them by individual landowners, county governments and community groups opposing particular segments.

  • Now — September 2026

    Commission decision and final order

    LCRA’s published schedule expects the route to be determined this month. The order is what turns a study area into a centerline, and it is the moment your tract either is or is not on the line.

  • After the order

    Survey permission requests

    Land agents ask for written permission to enter and survey. This is the first document most owners are asked to sign, it is negotiable, and it is where a great deal of leverage is given away for nothing.

  • After the survey

    Appraisal and the first written offer

    The company orders an appraisal and makes a written offer. Under Texas law that offer has to be a bona fide offer — a procedural standard, not a promise that the number is right.

  • If no agreement

    Condemnation and special commissioners

    The company files, three special commissioners are appointed, and they hold a hearing on value. Either side can object to the award and take it to court.

  • Summer 2030

    Target in service

    Oncor’s published target for completion.

Where the leverage actually is on a pending docket: you have two windows, not one. The first closes when the Commission signs the order and your tract is either on the route or off it. The second runs from survey permission to the written offer. Almost everything that determines your number happens in those two windows, and both of them close quietly.

The first offer is a starting point, not the number.

What a 765 kV easement costs you on Central Texas land

A 200-foot permanent easement across a mile of your property is a little over 24 acres inside the strip alone. On this corridor the strip is rarely the expensive part, because the land it crosses is worth more than rangeland and the things it damages are worth more than grass.

You keep the land and lose the use of it

A transmission easement is not a sale of the strip. You still own it, still pay taxes on it, and still cannot build on it, plant tall crops in it, or put permanent structures in it. On grazing land that costs little; on an orchard, a homesite, a lake-view tract or ground with a platting future, it costs a great deal.

Development potential is part of the value

In Williamson and Bell counties the market is not pricing grass. It is pricing what the tract will be in five or ten years. A 200-foot corridor across the buildable frontage can take the part of the property the whole price was based on, and an appraisal built on agricultural comparables will not show that. Highest and best use is a valuation question, and on this corridor it is often the whole argument.

Permanent plantings and orchards

San Saba and Mason counties carry pecan orchards, vineyards and other permanent plantings. Mature trees are not replaced by a per-acre figure. Establishment cost, lost production during re-establishment, and the effect on the rest of the block are all cost-to-cure and remainder items.

View, privacy and recreational value

Burnet and Llano county tracts sell on view, water and privacy. Steel lattice towers up to roughly 200 feet tall are visible for miles across Hill Country terrain. Where a buyer was paying for an unbroken ridgeline, the loss is real, it is measurable in comparable sales, and it belongs in damages to the remainder.

Hunting and lease income

Across Menard, McCulloch, Mason and Concho counties, hunting lease income is a documentable revenue stream. A corridor through a pasture affects the lease value of that pasture, not merely the acres inside the easement. If you have lease history, it is evidence.

Access, crossings and construction

Construction on a 765 kV line is heavy. Tower foundations, crane pads, pulling sites and access roads sit outside the permanent strip under a temporary construction easement that is negotiated separately and routinely priced as an afterthought. Where those roads cross your gates, ranch roads, cattle guards, fences and creek crossings, the terms belong in writing.

Field shape and irrigation

On the Blackland Prairie row-crop ground in Bell, Williamson and Milam, a corridor that cuts diagonally across a field costs you turn rows, efficiency and the shape of the remainder for as long as the line stands. That is a remainder damage, not a strip price.

What the offer will look like — and what it usually leaves out

Once the Commission signs an order and your tract is on the chosen route, the sequence is survey, appraisal, written offer. Under Texas Property Code Chapter 21 that first written offer has to be a bona fide offer supported by an appraisal. Bona fide is a procedural test. It does not mean the number is correct, and it does not mean the appraisal looked at everything it should have.

What just compensation is actually made of

ComponentWhat it pays forTypically
Value of the part acquiredThe strip itself, priced per acre against comparable salesLargest single line
Damages to the remainderLost value to everything you keep — severance, access, layout, irrigation, marketabilityFrequently understated
Cost to cureWhat it costs to fix what the taking broke — fences, gates, cattle guards, crossings, reseeding, drainageOften omitted entirely
Temporary construction easementExtra ground used during construction, separate from the permanent stripPriced as an afterthought

An offer that consists of one per-acre number multiplied by the acres in the strip has quietly priced three of those four lines at zero.

The appraisal that supports the offer was ordered and paid for by the party buying your land. That does not make it dishonest — it makes it one opinion of value, produced for someone whose interest is a low number. You are entitled to put a second opinion in front of them, and on Central Texas acreage the gap between the two is usually not small.

Before you sign anything, get these in writing

  • Exactly where the corridor crosses, and where the access roads and pulling sites will be
  • How many crossings you get, where they are, and who maintains them
  • What happens to fences, gates and cattle guards, and to what standard they are restored
  • Restoration terms for ruts, compaction, caliche, drainage and reseeding — with a time limit
  • Whether the easement language allows future lines, distribution taps, or third-party attachments on the same strip
  • How the temporary construction easement is priced and when it ends

What their offer may miss on this project

Eight things that are compensable, that landowners on Central Texas takings routinely are not paid for, and that a per-acre offer does not address.

Damage to the Remainder

A 200-foot high-voltage corridor can reduce the market value of your whole tract, not just the strip. On Central Texas acreage that effect is usually larger than the easement payment itself.

Development Potential

In Bell and Williamson counties the land is priced on what it will become. A corridor across the buildable frontage takes that, and an agricultural comparable will not show it.

Permanent Plantings

Pecan orchards and vineyards are decades of establishment. Replacement cost, lost production and the effect on the remaining block are all compensable and routinely omitted.

View and Privacy

Hill Country and Highland Lakes tracts sell on view. Towers up to roughly 200 feet tall across a ridgeline show up in comparable sales, not in a per-acre offer.

Access and Crossings

Where their trucks enter, how often, and what happens to your gates, ranch roads, cattle guards, fences and creek crossings during and after construction.

Temporary Construction Easement

Extra ground taken during construction — pads, pulling sites and access — negotiated separately from the permanent strip and routinely underpriced.

Lease Income You Lose

Hunting leases, grazing leases, and solar or wind options. A corridor across the tract affects the lease, not just the easement acres.

Your Ag Valuation

Texas Tax Code § 23.55(f) provides that rollback tax sanctions do not apply when the change of use results from a sale for right-of-way or from a condemnation. Confirm it with your own appraisal district — and ask separately what the corridor does to ag use on the remainder.

Questions landowners on this project are asking

Has a route been chosen for the Bell County East–Big Hill line?

No. As of September 9, 2026 there is no final order in PUCT Docket 59475, and Oncor’s own public mapping service still shows all 199 filed corridor links on this project as proposed rather than approved. LCRA’s published schedule expects the route to be determined in September 2026.

The intervention deadline passed in April. Did I lose my rights?

You lost party status in the routing case. You did not lose your right to just compensation. If the chosen route crosses your land, the company still has to make a bona fide written offer supported by an appraisal, and you can still negotiate it, reject it, and take it to special commissioners. Those are two different things and land agents sometimes let owners confuse them.

How wide is the easement and how tall are the towers?

Oncor’s published right-of-way width for 765 kV is approximately 200 feet. The structures are self-supporting steel lattice towers, typically 155 to 160 feet tall, with an estimated maximum of just under 200 feet. An ordinary distribution pole is about 34 feet above ground, so these are roughly five times that.

My land is near Georgetown or Temple. Is a suburban tract treated differently?

It should be. Bell and Williamson counties carry 95 of the 199 filed links and the most valuable land on the entire 765 kV program. On a tract whose price reflects development potential, the strip is a small part of the loss and the damage to the remainder is the main event. An appraisal built on agricultural comparables will systematically understate that, which is exactly the kind of offer worth having reviewed.

Can I refuse to let them survey my property?

Survey permission is a document you are asked to sign, and what it allows is negotiable — who comes, when, what they may do, what they must restore, and what notice you get. Signing the form as presented is the most common way a landowner gives up leverage before the conversation about money has even started.

I am in Schleicher or Tom Green County. Which docket am I in?

Possibly both. This line ends at Big Hill Substation, and the Big Hill–Sand Lake project, Docket 59182, begins at the same point. Landowners near Big Hill can sit inside two study areas at once, on two separate dockets, with two different sets of land agents. The docket number on the paperwork is what tells you which.

My county is named but I do not think a corridor reaches me. Should I do anything?

Coryell, Llano and Milam are named in the case style and no filed link reaches them — we measured all 199 published links against county boundaries. That does not make them irrelevant: corridors get combined and shifted between the filing and the final order. Checking your address against the studied corridors on this page takes a few seconds and costs nothing.
