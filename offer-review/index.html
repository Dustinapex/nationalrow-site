Five questions about how they handled you

Nothing here is an appraisal or an estimate of value — we can't price your land from a web page and neither can anyone else. These are simply the things we look at first.

Did their appraiser walk the property with you before pricing it?

Does the offer show a separate figure for damage to the land you keep?

Did they hand you a written copy of the appraisal it's based on?

Does it account for changed access, drainage, or fence you'll rebuild?

Was the land valued for what it could be used for — or only what it's used for now?

Five things not to do this week

None of this is legal advice. It is all about your own property and your own paperwork, and every one of them gets harder the longer you wait.

  • Don't sign anything yet, including a form that only lets them come survey.
  • Don't let their appraiser walk the property without you there.
  • Don't clear, mow, or move a fence until it is photographed.
  • Don't talk to the right-of-way agent without writing down afterwards who said what.
  • Don't throw away the envelope. The postmark is sometimes the only date on the whole thing.
Who you call matters

Your family attorney is excellent at wills.

He has probably never valued a partial take in his life. That's not a shot at him — it's a different job. Same reason you wouldn't ask us to write your will. We don't. We're not lawyers.

Almost every condemnation case is a fight about a number, not about the law.

The agency's right to take is usually not in question. What they owe you for it is. That's an appraisal argument — and appraisal is the whole job here.

Your general-practice attorney

Knows you. Has never done this.

Wills, closings, contracts. A partial take with remainder damages is something most general practices see once a decade, if ever.

An eminent domain attorney

Handles the law

Pleadings, procedure, the courtroom. If it becomes litigation, this is who you want. Good ones bring valuation people in.

If I need an attorney, do I have to go find one myself?

No. We give you options — owner-side condemnation attorneys matched to your county and project. You choose and engage them directly, so they work for you. You just don't start from a search engine with a deadline running.

What does it cost?

The review costs nothing and there is no obligation attached to it. If we take the case on we work on contingency, so you pay us only out of an increase we obtain — if the number never moves, you owe us nothing. Any attorney or appraiser you choose to engage sets their own fee with you directly.

Will fighting this make them take more of my land?

No. What's acquired is set by the project design, not by whether you question the price. Negotiating compensation doesn't change the footprint of the take.
