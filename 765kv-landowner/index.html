You got the letter. A condemnation notice is overwhelming by design.

It arrives thick. Legal language, a plat you cannot read, an appraisal you did not order, a number already filled in, and a date near the bottom that makes it feel decided. It is built so that signing feels like the reasonable thing to do.

  • “They want to walk my place.” The survey permission form is one of the few things in that packet you can still negotiate. Most people sign it first.
  • “Can they actually do this?” Once the PUCT grants the certificate, usually yes. What they pay you is the part still open.
  • “The number feels low and I have no way to know.” It came from an appraiser they hired. That is one opinion of value, not a verdict.
  • “This place has been in the family a long time.” A corridor changes access, fencing, spraying, where you can build, and what the rest of it is worth.

You are not stuck. The next few decisions just need to be made with better information than the envelope gave you.

What a first offer usually leaves out

  • Damage to the remainderA 200-foot corridor does not just take the acres under it. It splits fields, strands corners, moves your building envelope and follows you into every future appraisal of the whole tract.
  • Access and crossingsWhere they can enter, how often, which roads they build, who maintains them, and whether you can still cross your own easement with equipment.
  • Temporary construction easementWork space outside the permanent corridor is a separate taking with its own value, and it is frequently folded in for free.
  • Restoration after they leaveRuts, gates, cattle guards, fences, reseeding, and the drainage their equipment changed. Who repairs what, to what standard, and for how long belongs in the easement in writing — not in a handshake at the gate.
  • Your ag valuationTexas Tax Code § 23.55(f) says rollback sanctions do not apply when the change of use comes from a condemnation or a sale for right-of-way — worth confirming with your appraisal district before anyone tells you otherwise. What an offer rarely deals with is what the corridor does to the ag use on everything you keep.
  • The easement language itselfWidth, height, vegetation control, future additional circuits, assignment to third parties. The document outlives the check by decades.
